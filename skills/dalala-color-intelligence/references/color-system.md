# Role-based colour system

## Brief translation

Convert vague direction into observable constraints:

- temperature: warm, cool, neutral, controlled contrast
- lightness: pale, middle, deep, wide range
- saturation: muted, clean, vivid, gray-toned, ceremonial
- cultural signal: historical, editorial, contemporary, youthful, premium, technical, festive
- risk: cliché, nostalgia, low readability, excessive decoration, weak hierarchy, cheap saturation

Resolve conflicts through roles. “Young and classical” may use a contemporary neutral canvas, one historical anchor, and one clean accent instead of making every colour antique.

## Role architecture

- canvas or background: largest area and environmental tone
- primary text: strongest reading contrast
- secondary text: quieter but still readable
- subject support: connects the palette to image or product without swallowing it
- accent: one high-attention role for a decisive word, data point, CTA, seal, or transition
- structural: border, grid, divider, inactive data, or shadow
- semantic: success, warning, danger, information, selection only when the surface needs them

## Default area starting points

These are starting ranges, not fixed recipes.

- cover or advertisement: dominant 65–80%, support 15–25%, accent 3–10%
- poster: dominant 55–75%, support 20–35%, accent 3–12%
- PPT or resume: canvas 70–88%, text and structure 10–24%, accent 2–8%
- UI: canvas and surfaces 75–90%, text and borders 8–20%, action or status 2–8%
- chart: background and axes stay quiet; data colours follow categorical, sequential, diverging, or highlight logic

Change ratios when imagery already contributes strong colour. Count the visible image as part of the palette.

## Strategy meanings

- restrained: same-family, gray-toned, neutral, or light-dark relations; one modest accent
- identity: one recognisable anchor with quiet support and strict repetition rules
- contrast: complementary or split-complementary relation with limited area and clear hierarchy
- warm-cool: temperature opposition separates protagonist from field or one content group from another
- light-dark: value contrast carries the hierarchy when hue should remain calm
- series: stable canvas and text roles plus controlled rotating accents
- data: choose by data semantics, distinguishability, and monotonic value where relevant
- print: choose by material, ink coverage, finish, shelf context, and proofing risk

## Failure checks

- the prettiest colour occupies every role
- title, decoration, CTA, and subject all use competing accents
- body text passes mood but fails contrast
- palette fights the supplied photograph's dominant colours
- low-saturation colours collapse into one muddy value
- traditional names replace actual design reasoning
- a reference palette is copied because the same layout was selected
