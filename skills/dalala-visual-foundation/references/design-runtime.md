# Internal Design Runtime

Use this file to convert a brief and its assets into one coherent visual decision. Keep the state internal unless the user requests an explanation.

## 1. Build the design state

Resolve these fields before drawing or typesetting:

- `communication_job`: stop, trust, understand, compare, remember, or act. Choose one primary verb.
- `audience_state`: what the viewer already knows, fears, wants, or is trying to decide.
- `memory_anchor`: the single word, number, face, object, or conclusion that must survive at thumbnail size.
- `semantic_topology`: one point, two-way contrast, hierarchy, sequence, causal path, system, group, expansion, enclosure, or spatial journey.
- `evidence`: what must remain visibly true for the message to be credible.
- `subject_map`: number of subjects, bounding areas, visual weight, gaze, gesture, motion, depth, and recognition-critical parts.
- `field_map`: high-detail zones, low-detail zones, natural axes, light-dark boundaries, perspective lines, depth planes, and crop risks.
- `text_load`: title length, natural phrase breaks, number of essential levels, and reading speed.
- `canvas_pressure`: whether the target ratio creates horizontal, vertical, central, edge, or depth pressure.
- `dominant_force`: the one structural force that should organize the frame.
- `dominant_contrast`: choose one of size, light-dark, colour, sharp-soft, dense-sparse, near-far, still-moving, or open-closed.

If `memory_anchor`, `evidence`, or `dominant_force` is unclear, the design is not ready.

## 2. Decide the reading path

Design the first three fixations, not a collection of objects:

1. `entry`: the first unavoidable cue.
2. `meaning`: the cue that explains what the first cue means.
3. `proof_or_exit`: the evidence, identity, action, or open direction that completes the frame.

For a cover, three fixations are usually enough. For a slide, the third fixation may lead into a chart, diagram, or next build. Any element outside this path must justify its presence.

## 3. Choose the structural relationship

Choose one primary relationship:

- `isolate`: protect one decisive subject with quiet surrounding space.
- `counterweight`: place type or evidence opposite a weighted subject.
- `follow`: align reading with gaze, gesture, road, light, perspective, or motion.
- `converge`: make several forces point to one answer.
- `contrast`: maintain a meaningful two-sided difference.
- `sequence`: create visible order, stages, or changing scale.
- `contain`: use a real or semantic boundary to create focus, safety, pressure, or exclusion.
- `reveal`: use foreground, depth, cropping, or open/closed space to disclose information progressively.
- `field`: allow atmosphere or environment to be the primary carrier while the subject provides scale or evidence.

Do not combine two primary relationships unless one is clearly subordinate.

## 4. Allocate visual budget

Treat attention as finite.

- One element receives `dominant` weight.
- One or two elements may receive `supporting` weight.
- Everything else is `ambient`, `evidence`, or removed.
- Do not make title, portrait, colour block, icon, and badge all dominant.
- Increase impact by increasing difference, not by increasing every object's size.

The title does not automatically deserve dominance. In trust content the face or real evidence may lead; in a strong opinion the title may lead; in spatial content the environment may lead.

## 5. Retrieve, do not imitate

Use the 100 composition cards to retrieve a force relationship after the state is known. Preserve the selected precedent's force, reading order, density gradient, and whitespace duty—not its coordinates, decorative forms, or legacy 3:4 container.

Generate three structurally different candidates only when the best relationship is not obvious. Hard-reject any candidate that violates identity, evidence, gaze space, native line breaks, or target-ratio pressure.

## 6. Finish the surface after structure

Only after the composition is stable, choose:

- one aesthetic family;
- one material logic;
- palette roles: climate, structure, story, accent;
- one type behaviour;
- at most one accent technique.

Surface style may reinforce the message but may not repair a weak structure.
