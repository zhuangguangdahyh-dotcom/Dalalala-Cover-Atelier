# Universal Visual Grammar distilled from the 350-reference library

## What was studied

All fourteen contact sheets covering references 001–350 were visually reviewed. The library spans photographic composition, traditional Chinese spatial structures, perspective and depth, grids and typography, editorial and advertising pages, web and UI information structures, film framing, reading patterns, and presentation pages.

The source works are trusted examples of designed communication. Local deep-study status measures how completely each example has been converted into executable rules; it does not grade the source's quality.

## The seven decisions that create a designed page

1. **Communication job** — identify whether the page must attract, explain, compare, prove, sequence, navigate, persuade, or pause.
2. **Visual protagonist** — choose the one thing that carries the promise: a face, product, number, sentence, chart, scene, symbol, or empty field.
3. **Spatial skeleton** — choose an axis, field, grid, frame, path, layer, split, cluster, or depth system that matches the job.
4. **Mass distribution** — decide where the largest dark/light, dense/empty, large/small, sharp/soft masses sit. Small labels cannot repair an unresolved mass balance.
5. **Reading motion** — design a visible entry, one or two transitions, and an exit or resting point. Alignment, gaze, direction, repetition, numbering, and contrast create this motion.
6. **Content relationship** — make image, title, evidence, labels, and decoration interact. Each element must lead, support, counterweight, contain, connect, or separate.
7. **Surface language** — choose type, colour, material, texture, and detail after the previous six decisions. Surface language amplifies the structure.

## Twelve transferable template families

### 1. Focal field

One dominant subject sits at a high-value intersection or against a large field. Use for a single claim, portrait, product, or emotional scene. Evidence includes thirds, golden ratio, centred, offset, visual-centre, isolation, and primary-secondary relations.

### 2. Directed axis

A diagonal, curve, S path, radial path, converging perspective, or gaze moves the eye. Use for action, conflict, change, progress, journey, and before-to-after logic. The subject and title must participate in the direction.

### 3. Framed window

Content is revealed through a frame, crop, aperture, image window, cutout, or nested boundary. Use for discovery, selection, focus, case evidence, and editorial refinement. The frame controls attention and depth.

### 4. Split comparison

Two or more fields establish contrast, dialogue, choice, before/after, cause/effect, or competing viewpoints. Weight need not be equal, but comparison criteria must align.

### 5. Modular evidence

Cards, grids, panels, tables, galleries, and dashboards turn many items into comparable units. Use for proof, lists, portfolios, metrics, features, and structured knowledge. Modules need one governing rhythm and one priority exception.

### 6. Layered depth

Foreground, middle ground, background, overlays, floating blocks, and occlusion establish sequence and atmosphere. Use for immersive stories, technology, space, filmic scenes, and complex information with a clear anchor.

### 7. Typographic stage

Type carries the page as image: headline-led, vertical, shaped, path-based, asymmetric, justified, oversized-number, quotation, or letterform layout. Use when the sentence or number is the strongest asset.

### 8. Negative-shape tension

An active empty shape, figure-ground reversal, dense-sparse opposition, or hidden-reveal relation creates meaning. Use for restraint, wit, premium mood, suspense, and conceptual advertising. Empty space must have a recognisable contour and job.

### 9. Editorial narrative

Columns, sidebars, margin notes, spreads, chapter openers, catalogues, indexes, and wraparound images organise reading over time. Use for resumes, reports, PPT sections, long graphics, and brand stories.

### 10. Sequential flow

Timelines, processes, stairs, carousels, accordions, branches, and vertical/horizontal streams encode progression. Use when order changes meaning. The path must remain legible before detailed text is read.

### 11. System map

Axes, matrices, radial systems, networks, clusters, trees, and maps explain relationships. Use for strategy, frameworks, portfolios, and complex decisions. The visual topology must match the real relationship.

### 12. Monumental symbol

A large geometric or figurative symbol becomes the architecture of the page. Use for title slides, campaigns, posters, and strong brand statements. Secondary information stays subordinate to the symbol.

## Anti-generic test

Hide all small labels and decoration. If the visual protagonist, major masses, and reading route no longer express the intended job, the structure was never designed. If only colours and stickers distinguish two variants, they are one layout with several superficial skins.
