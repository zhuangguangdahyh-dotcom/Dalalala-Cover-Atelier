# Canvas Composition Engine

Aspect ratio changes the available forces. Preserve relationships, not coordinates.

## Universal canvas model

Divide the canvas conceptually into:

- `core`: where the memory anchor can survive cropping and thumbnail view;
- `support`: where title, proof, identity, or secondary image can clarify the core;
- `breath`: space that protects focus or direction;
- `edge`: space used for scale, tension, continuation, or controlled cropping;
- `unsafe`: interface overlays, platform crops, or regions too close to trim.

Do not fill every zone. A strong composition often leaves one or two zones intentionally unused.

## 3:4 and 4:5 social covers

- Vertical stacking creates hierarchy quickly, so resist turning every cover into top title / middle face / bottom caption.
- Use side-to-side counterweight when the portrait or object is off-centre.
- A title of two semantic beats can occupy a vertical rhythm; do not split each Chinese character into its own line merely to fill height.
- Keep the memory anchor inside the upper two-thirds unless the lower placement is part of a deliberate weight or reveal.
- Edges may carry cropped objects or large type, but the centre must remain readable at mobile thumbnail size.

## 9:16 vertical video or story

- The height should express time, movement, distance, scale, or layered depth—not simply more empty background.
- Reserve interface-safe space without building visible empty bands.
- Use a strong upper or middle entry point and let the lower field carry progression, evidence, or exit.
- Long vertical type is acceptable only when its cadence and meaning support vertical reading.

## 16:9 presentation and video thumbnail

- Horizontal space encourages a relationship: subject–statement, problem–evidence, before–after, or source–destination.
- Avoid default equal halves. A 60/40 or 70/30 imbalance usually creates clearer authorship unless equality is the message.
- A horizontal title should have a controlled line length; use two lines only when the break creates meaning or counterweight.
- Preserve an obvious slide edge and safe margin; do not let supporting labels create a second navigation system.

## 1:1 square

- The square naturally pulls toward the centre. Introduce a deliberate axis, offset, edge crop, diagonal, or depth cue when static centring would weaken the message.
- Use fewer elements; the square becomes crowded faster than vertical or wide formats.

## Multi-page carousel

- Keep stable type, palette, material, and margin behaviour across the set.
- Vary silhouette, scale, density, direction, and page function so the set does not become one template with replaced content.
- Each page should make one information advance. The cover promises; later pages prove, complicate, compare, or resolve it.
- Series rhythm should alternate pressure and breath. Consecutive pages should not all use the same centre, crop, or text block size.

## Slide decks

- Establish a layout grammar, not a single master: title, statement, evidence, comparison, process, data, and closing slides may require different structures.
- Keep one visual thesis per slide. Move detail to builds or later slides instead of shrinking everything.
- Use alignment and repetition to create deck identity, then allow controlled breaks for high-emphasis slides.
- A deck is judged both slide-by-slide and as a contact sheet. Repeated silhouettes signal template dependence.
