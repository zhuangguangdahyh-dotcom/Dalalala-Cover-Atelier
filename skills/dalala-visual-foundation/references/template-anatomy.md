# Template Anatomy

Analyse a reference as a content-and-space system before adapting it.

## Required anatomy

- **Canvas field:** ratio, orientation, safe edge, dominant open shape, density map.
- **Primary subject:** content role, bounding mass, position, crop, direction, depth, contrast.
- **Main title:** semantic groups, line count, total silhouette, scale ratio, alignment, rotation, overlap.
- **Secondary text:** what it adds, where it attaches, and why it stays subordinate.
- **Evidence:** image, data, quotation, feature, or label that makes the claim believable.
- **Decoration:** exact structural job and attachment point.
- **Reading path:** entry, transition, stop, and optional exit.
- **Locked relations:** the minimum relationships that make the template recognisable.
- **Adaptive parts:** content-sensitive sizes, crop, spacing, and optional modules.
- **Rejection conditions:** content or assets that break the grammar.

## Measure relations, not isolated coordinates

Record approximate proportions such as subject area to canvas, title width to subject width, large field to dense field, overlap percentage, margin rhythm, line-length contrast, and module cadence. When adapting to another format, preserve these relations while rebuilding coordinates.

## Match by content anatomy

Before choosing a template, count the real roles: number of protagonists, title length and semantic groups, subtitle need, evidence count, sequence length, comparison sides, and required brand or legal data. Choose a template whose native slots match these roles. Do not invent filler content to occupy a reference's decorative labels.

## Locked contract

Lock one dominant skeleton, focus, reading path, mass distribution, and image-text relationship. Colour, font family, texture, copy length, crop amount, and optional support modules remain adaptive unless the selected rule explicitly depends on them.
