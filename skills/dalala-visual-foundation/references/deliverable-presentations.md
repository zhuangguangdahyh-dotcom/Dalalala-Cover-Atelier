# Presentation Design Runtime

Use with the presentation-production Skill. The presentation tool handles file creation; this file controls visual judgment.

## Start from the deck argument

Before slide design, identify:

- what the audience should believe or decide at the end;
- the sequence of claims needed to get there;
- which claims require proof, comparison, process, scale, or emotional reset;
- where the deck needs compression and where it needs breath.

Slide structure follows the argument. Do not distribute content evenly merely to fill a requested slide count.

## Give each slide a role

Use roles such as:

- `orientation`: where we are and why it matters;
- `claim`: one decisive judgment;
- `evidence`: image, quotation, case, chart, or number;
- `mechanism`: relationship, system, or causal model;
- `sequence`: stages, timeline, or journey;
- `comparison`: a meaningful difference with a chosen basis;
- `synthesis`: several threads converge into one conclusion;
- `reset`: visual breath, emotional transition, or chapter boundary;
- `decision`: recommendation, next step, or commitment.

The role determines the composition. Do not force every role into title + three cards.

## Deck-level rhythm

- Alternate dense evidence with clear claims or visual resets.
- Repeat a small set of alignment, type, and colour behaviours, not one identical layout.
- Use large-scale changes sparingly to mark chapter shifts or decisive conclusions.
- Maintain a recognisable visual centre on every slide.
- On a contact sheet, adjacent slides should show controlled variety in silhouette, density, dominant position, and image scale.

## Image and diagram use

- Use one strong image when it carries atmosphere, evidence, or a spatial relationship.
- Use diagrams only when relationships are easier to understand visually than in a short sentence.
- Charts must have an explicit takeaway and visual emphasis; default software styling is not a design decision.
- Avoid decorative icon grids, repeated rounded cards, generic stock-photo bands, and oversized chapter numbers without semantic value.

## Text discipline

- One slide, one thesis.
- Titles should state the point, not merely name the topic.
- Move secondary reasoning to a later slide instead of shrinking it.
- A paragraph is acceptable when slow reading is the intended experience; otherwise convert it into hierarchy, sequence, contrast, or evidence.
- Keep annotations close to what they explain.

## Presentation QA

Render the entire deck and inspect:

- each slide at full size for cropping, typography, and evidence;
- the full montage for repeated silhouettes, monotony, and abrupt stylistic drift;
- the deck in sequence for narrative pacing and visual resets;
- the first, last, and decision slides at thumbnail size for recognisable hierarchy.
