# Graphics and Cover Runtime

Use for video covers, social covers, posters, single graphics, and carousel pages.

## Cover priority

At thumbnail scale preserve, in order:

1. the click reason or memory anchor;
2. the recognisable person, object, result, or environment;
3. the relationship between them;
4. only then identity, category, episode, or decorative metadata.

The title and image must answer the same question from different directions. If both merely repeat the same noun, the cover lacks tension.

## Choose who leads

- `person-led`: trust, emotion, expertise, testimony, conflict, or transformation depends on the real person.
- `title-led`: a short judgment, number, contradiction, or question is stronger than the available image.
- `evidence-led`: a case, object, diagram, result, or spatial condition proves the point.
- `environment-led`: location, atmosphere, scale, or context is the message; the person becomes scale or witness.

Do not enlarge a face by habit. Do not hide a useful face merely to look conceptual.

## One concentrated hook

Choose one primary hook:

- semantic contradiction;
- unexpected crop or scale;
- decisive expression or action;
- controlled colour break;
- meaningful obstruction or reveal;
- strong empty-space tension;
- an image–title counterpoint.

Avoid adding arrows, stickers, glow, multiple accent colours, several hooks, or false urgency after the structure is already clear.

## Carousel rhythm

- Cover: compress the promise.
- Early page: clarify the situation or stakes.
- Middle pages: change the form as the reasoning changes—comparison, sequence, evidence, mechanism, or case.
- Final page: resolve or open a next action; do not repeat the cover at lower energy.

The set must feel related without using the same composition on every page.
