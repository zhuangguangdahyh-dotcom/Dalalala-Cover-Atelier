# Typography Engine

Typography carries hierarchy, cadence, voice, and spatial weight. It is not the final layer added after the image.

## Build hierarchy from function

Every visible text element must be one of:

- `anchor`: the phrase remembered first;
- `clarifier`: what the anchor means;
- `evidence`: number, identity, source, or proof;
- `navigation`: section, page, or sequence cue;
- `ambient`: restrained editorial texture that can disappear without losing meaning.

Most covers need only anchor plus one evidence or identity line. Most slides need anchor, clarifier, and one evidence region. Remove text that has no function.

## Chinese line breaking

- Break at complete semantic beats, contrasts, verbs, or intentional suspense.
- Keep modifiers with the noun or verb they qualify unless separation creates the intended emphasis.
- Do not orphan punctuation, particles, units, or one low-value character.
- Do not force equal line lengths when unequal lines create a better silhouette and more natural speech.
- A number and its unit normally remain together.
- If a title needs more than three lines at thumbnail scale, shorten the copy or change the composition.

## Weight and scale

- Establish hierarchy with one decisive scale jump, not several medium differences.
- A supporting line should normally feel clearly subordinate rather than only 10% smaller.
- Large type requires surrounding silence. If it touches several other strong elements, it loses authority and becomes noise.
- Use boldness for structure or urgency, not as a universal readability fix.
- Thin editorial type needs enough size, contrast, and quiet background to survive rendering.

## Alignment

- Align to image structure, not an invisible generic grid alone.
- Left alignment supports scanning and explanation; centred alignment supports ceremony or singularity; right alignment can create counterweight or delayed entry.
- Mixed alignments are allowed only when they express hierarchy or movement.
- Baselines, edge alignments, and repeated gaps should create a rhythm the viewer can feel without seeing guides.

## Spacing

- Tight tracking increases density and authority; open tracking increases atmosphere and object-like presence. Neither is automatically premium.
- Chinese titles generally need optical, not mechanical, spacing—especially around punctuation, Latin text, and numerals.
- Line spacing follows reading tempo: compressed for a single graphic shape, open for reflective or explanatory reading.
- Keep title-to-image distance intentional. A near distance implies attachment; a larger gap implies commentary or independence.

## Type and material

- Match the type behaviour to the visual material: precise type can contrast rough media; handmade type must share the actual mark-making logic rather than use a novelty font.
- Do not mix type families merely to look editorial. Every family needs a role.
- Decorative English, labels, page numbers, and fine rules are ambient. They must never become the reason the design appears styled.

## Readability tests

- At thumbnail size, the anchor remains readable or at least visually identifiable.
- At normal size, the complete meaning and hierarchy are clear.
- At close view, spacing, punctuation, baselines, and material edges remain intentional.
- If readability requires outline + shadow + glow, relocate or simplify the type.
